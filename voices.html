Voices

First-person testimonies from parents whose children were taken — shared anonymously to protect the families.

Behind every case is a parent living with the loss of their child. These are their words, in their own voice. Every testimony is published anonymously: we remove names, dates, locations, and any detail that could identify the family or the child before anything goes online.

Share your story. If your child was taken through parental abduction involving Japan, you can add your voice. Email us at info@japanabductionwatch.org or use our report form. Tell us as much or as little as you want — we will work with you to anonymize it, and nothing is published without your approval.

Testimony of a left-behind parent

My company sent me to the United States on an overseas assignment. My wife and our baby daughter came with me. We built a life there — a home, playdates for our daughter, a daily routine.

One day, without my knowledge or consent, my daughter's mother took her from the United States back to Japan. I later learned it had been planned in secret: she knew I had refused, and she left while I was unaware. Our daughter was still only a small child.

I did everything the law allows. I filed for my daughter's return under the Hague Convention — in Japan. The court dismissed my petition, and my appeal was rejected. The courts ruled that my daughter's “habitual residence” was Japan — not the home where she had actually been living — and so she would not be returned.

In all the time since, I have seen my daughter only once: supervised, for a short while, in a lawyers' building in Japan, because the other side required a lawyer to be present. Since that single visit, I have had no contact with her at all.

Japan is a party to the Hague Convention, yet return orders are rarely enforced, and there is still no penalty for the act of taking a child. The other side once promised, in writing, that I would be allowed to see her. That promise was never kept.

My daughter is growing up without knowing her father. I am not telling this story for revenge. I am telling it so people understand this is not a private tragedy, but a systemic one.

— A left-behind parent
