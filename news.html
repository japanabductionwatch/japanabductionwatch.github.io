News

A running record of developments in parental child abduction involving Japan — law, courts, international pressure, and the families affected.

This page is updated regularly with the most significant news we can find, in English and Japanese, each item linked to its source so you can read the original. It is a summary for orientation, not legal advice.

Last updated: 2 November 2026

23 October 2026 · Law and politics

Government tells the Diet that taking a child without the other parent’s consent “may constitute” spousal violence

At a sitting of the House of Representatives Cabinet Committee on 23 October, lawmaker Koichiro Ichimura asked the government whether taking a child away without the other spouse’s consent amounts to violence. A Cabinet Office bureau chief, Keiko Sawai, replied that it depends on the case, but that where taking a child without consent is found to have harmed the other parent’s mind or body, there is a possibility it falls within “violence by a spouse” as defined in Article 1(1) of the Act on the Prevention of Spousal Violence. A Ministry of Justice official, Toshinori Takebayashi, added that if one parent moves a child without the other’s knowledge and for no particular reason, and that is judged to be domestic violence under the Act, the Ministry has no objection to treating the other parent as its victim. The exchange does not change the law, and the Act concerns protection orders and support measures rather than the return of a child.

Sources: Chanto Kyodo Shinken Online (23 October 2026) · House of Representatives, sittings scheduled for 23 October 2026

14 October 2026 · Affected family

A petition over how separated parents are described: NPO Kimito asks the government to press news organisations to explain the domestic-violence support measure correctly

NPO Kimito began collecting signatures on 7 October for a petition to the Minister for Internal Affairs and Communications, asking the ministry to press newspapers and broadcasters to describe the residence-concealment support measure accurately. The group argues that when reports use the words “victim” and “perpetrator” for a measure that a municipal counter grants on application, readers take it for a finding of domestic violence, and the other parent is treated accordingly. It points to a suit brought against the town of Shiroishi, Saga prefecture, by a man in his fifties from Kanagawa whose address was withheld from him as a domestic-violence perpetrator; the town settled the case with an apology after police found no need for the measure. The group also cites its own 2024 survey of 509 separated parents, in which about 23% said such a measure had been taken against them, and a Nagoya High Court judgment recognising harm where a parent could not correct the information held about them. Kimito says it has approached lawmakers and arranged a meeting with a member of the House of Representatives.

Sources: NPO Kimito via PR TIMES (14 October 2026) · Nishinippon Shimbun, on the Shiroishi settlement and apology · Chanto Kyodo Shinken Online on the same case (10 April 2026)

14 October 2026 · Affected family

Tokyo state-liability suit: a closed session on 9 October, and plaintiffs at the courthouse press club — “even after the Civil Code was amended, the taking of children by a parent is left unchecked”

About thirty men and women whose children were taken by a spouse, and who can no longer see them, sued the state at the Tokyo District Court in March, arguing that the Diet’s failure to legislate against the removal of a child violated constitutional rights including parental authority and custody; each claims ¥50,000 in damages. According to Chanto Kyodo Shinken Online, a preparatory session for oral argument was held at the court on 9 October behind closed doors, and four plaintiffs went to the courthouse press club the same day to describe the case, with their lead counsel Satoshi Sakka joining by telephone; the date of the next hearing was still being arranged. The plaintiffs say that even after the Civil Code was amended, the taking of children by a parent is left unchecked. It is a separate case from the suit brought at the Osaka District Court in July by twenty-seven parents.

Sources: Chanto Kyodo Shinken Online (14 October 2026) · Kyodo via Ryukyu Shimpo (7 March 2026)

9 October 2026 · Law in practice

Government tabulation: an arrangement for parent-child contact was recorded in 75.8% of April–June divorce registrations, up about 17 points

The Ministry of Justice has published its quarterly tabulation of the checkboxes on divorce-registration forms, and Justice Minister Takashi Yamashita gave the headline figures at a press conference on 9 October. The quarter, April to June 2026, is the first full quarter under the amended Civil Code. Among divorces by mutual agreement involving minor children, 75.8% of forms recorded that the parents had settled arrangements for parent-child contact and 74.7% that they had settled child support, up from 58.9% and 57.6% in January–March. A third box, on sharing the day-to-day raising of the child, was added in April and was ticked in 70.3% of cases. The child-support figure clears the government’s 70% target for the first time since it was set in 2020. The figures are provisional and record what parents entered on the form rather than whether contact is actually taking place.

Sources: Ministry of Justice, tabulation of divorce-registration checkboxes (PDF, October 2026) · Ministry of Justice page · Jiji Press (9 October 2026) · Kyodo via Daily Sports (9 October 2026)

29 September 2026 · Law in practice

First national figures since the reform: joint custody for 24.7% of the children whose parents divorced in April

The Health, Labour and Welfare Ministry’s preliminary monthly vital statistics for April 2026, published on 8 September, are the first national data covering divorces registered since the amended Civil Code took effect on 1 April. Of the 12,558 minor children whose parents divorced that month, 24.7% came under the joint custody of both parents and 67.3% under the mother’s sole custody, with the rest under the father’s sole custody; NHK summarised the result as roughly a quarter. The figures cover a single month and record what was entered on divorce registrations rather than whether a parent and child are in contact, and they do not capture children taken while a marriage is still in place. The ministry’s May report, published on 6 October, recorded 13,154 divorces in the month, 6.8% fewer than in May 2025.

Sources: Nikkei (29 September 2026) · NHK (3 October 2026) · MHLW, Monthly Vital Statistics (preliminary), April 2026 · MHLW, Monthly Vital Statistics (preliminary), May 2026 (published 6 October 2026)

28 September 2026 · International

U.S. announces visa restrictions on officials who obstruct the resolution of international child-abduction cases

Secretary of State Marco Rubio announced a new policy restricting visas for individuals “responsible for, or complicit in, impeding the prompt resolution of international parental child abduction cases,” and their immediate family members. The statement lists “foreign government officials, judges, judicial administrators, law enforcement officials, and others” who intentionally obstruct such cases or fail to implement laws, policies or court orders to resolve them and return children to their country of habitual residence, and describes the policy as a new accountability tool to press non-compliant countries to meet their obligations, taken under Section 212(a)(3)(C) of the Immigration and Nationality Act. The statement names no country, Japan among them, and does not say whether the policy has been applied to anyone; the State Department’s 2026 annual report did not cite Japan for a pattern of noncompliance.

Sources: U.S. Department of State, press statement by Secretary Marco Rubio (28 September 2026) · 2026 Annual Report on International Child Abduction (PDF)

17 September 2026 · International

Japan briefs the European Parliament’s Japan delegation in Strasbourg; its children’s-rights coordinator calls the reform a first step whose implementation must be watched

At the European Parliament’s request, Japan’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs briefed the Parliament’s Delegation for Relations with Japan in Strasbourg on 17 September, with the Ministry of Justice taking part online. The delegation’s chair Adam Bielan, twelve other members, and Vice-President Ewa Kopacz, the Parliament’s Coordinator for Children’s Rights, attended. The foreign ministry presented data on Japan’s implementation of the 1980 Hague Convention, including returns of children and the facilitation of parent-child contact, and the justice ministry explained the 2024 Civil Code amendments that took effect in April 2026. According to the ministry’s account, Kopacz welcomed the amendment as a first step by an important partner and said the law’s implementation must be followed so that joint custody and parent-child contact are secured in practice.

Source: Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Japan, press release (18 September 2026)

11 July 2026 · International

CNN: Japan’s joint-custody reform is in force, but the taking of a child was left out of it

CNN reported on the Civil Code reform that took effect in April 2026 and on the parents still fighting to see their children. The network noted that the reform does not make the act of taking a child an offence, and quoted a lawyer who expects parental abductions to continue because the issue was left out of the amendment.

Source: CNN (11 July 2026)

9 July 2026 · Affected parents

Osaka state-liability suit: 27 separated parents sue, calling the absence of any ban on taking a child “unconstitutional”

Twenty-seven men and women from seven prefectures sued the state at the Osaka District Court on 6 July, arguing that its failure to legislate for shared parenting and adequate parent-child contact severed the parent-child bond in breach of the Constitution; each claims ¥30,000 in damages. The complaint rests on the Convention on the Rights of the Child and Article 13 of the Constitution, and the suit was organised by the lawyer Masafumi Kawamura. It is a separate case from the suit brought by about thirty parents at the Tokyo District Court in March.

Sources: Jiji Press (9 July 2026) · Sankei Shimbun, MBS News and Kyodo News (9 July 2026)

17 June 2026 · Affected family

A national wire feature that starts with the families: a grandmother in Kawasaki, a son who took his own life, and a grandchild she cannot see

Kyodo News published a feature on 17 June 2026, carried by regional papers including the Hokkaido Shimbun and the Kumamoto Nichinichi Shimbun, on parents and grandparents cut off from children after one parent moved out with them. It opens with a 78-year-old woman in Kawasaki, identified only as “A,” whose eldest son died by suicide in 2024 at the age of 47, eight months after his wife took their son, now eleven; the article shows her holding a letter the grandchild wrote. It also pictures Kazuki Nakamura, described as a lead plaintiff, reading a letter from a child, and reports the view that lawyers and municipalities coach parents through the procedures involved and that the situation is “internationally abnormal.”

Sources: Hokkaido Shimbun (17 June 2026) · Kyodo News via Kumamoto Nichinichi Shimbun (17 June 2026)
