News

A running record of developments in parental child abduction involving Japan — law, courts, international pressure, and the families affected.

This page is updated regularly with the most significant news we can find, in English and Japanese, each item linked to its source so you can read the original. It is a summary for orientation, not legal advice.

Last updated: 27 August 2026

1 April 2026 · Law reform

Japan's joint-custody law takes effect, ending a century of sole custody

A revision to Japan's Civil Code allowing joint parental custody after divorce came into force on 1 April 2026 — the first time in roughly 80 years, and the end of a system under which courts had granted sole custody in essentially every post-divorce case. Supporters call it overdue; critics warn about risks for domestic-violence survivors. Crucially, the reform still carries no penalty for the act of taking a child.

Sources: CNN · Metropolis Japan

2026 · Hague Convention

Joint custody may strengthen “rights of custody” in Hague return cases

Practitioners note that once both parents hold parental authority, taking a child abroad without the other's consent may more clearly amount to wrongful removal under Article 3 of the Hague Convention. Reported data for 2017–2025 show Tokyo and Osaka family courts issuing return orders in about 70% of cases at first instance (63% on appeal) — above the international average — though enforcement and domestic cases remain the harder problem.

Source: Multi-Jurisdictional Divorce

7 July 2025 · Court ruling (France)

Paris court sentences a parent to 2 years and strips custody over a Japan abduction

A Paris court sentenced the Japanese ex-wife of French father Vincent Fichot to two years' imprisonment and removed her parental authority for taking their two children from their Tokyo home without his consent and cutting off contact — a rare instance of a national court treating cross-border parental abduction as a crime.

Source: coki

15 February 2026 · Affected family

An Italian father's children vanish in Japan, reigniting the debate

Michele Dall'Arno, an Italian lecturer in Japan, says his children disappeared after his separation — the start of what he describes as an emotional and bureaucratic nightmare, with little swift recourse for the excluded parent. His case has renewed public debate over how Japan handles parental abduction, and how it affects the parents and children left behind.

Source: International Reporters

Ongoing · International pressure

EU and US pressure on Japan continues

The European Parliament's 2020 resolution (adopted 686 votes to 1) urging Japan to enforce return orders and adopt shared custody remains a reference point, and EU lawmakers have continued to press Japan on the abduction of children of EU nationals. The U.S. State Department's annual reports have historically cited Japan, and the issue remains on the diplomatic agenda.

Sources: European Parliament · Reuters via Yahoo
